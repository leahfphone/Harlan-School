4.1 The Eastern Coasts and Mountains

The eastern United States includes the Eastern, Middle, and Western United States regions. Forests cover much of the East. The Appalachian Mountains stretch from Alabama toward Canada. The Appalachian region includes the Green Mountains, White Mountains, Catskills, Adirondacks, Alleghenies, Blue Ridge Mountains, and Cumberland Mountains. Mammoth Cave in Kentucky is a large cave system. The Piedmont lies east of the Appalachians, and the Atlantic Coastal Plain runs along the coast.

4.2 The Atlantic Ocean

The Atlantic Ocean is the world's second-largest ocean. It covers about one-fifth of Earth's surface. Ocean currents help move water and influence climates. Fish, lobsters, and other sea life provide food and jobs. Water temperature changes with latitude and depth.

4.3 Woodland Animals

The Eastern Woodlands provided Native Americans with food, shelter, and clothing. Fish and shellfish were plentiful along the Atlantic Coast. Wild turkeys and deer lived in the woodlands. Wampum was made from shells and used for gifts, records, and agreements. Black bears and white-tailed deer also lived in this region.

4.4 Indians of the Eastern Woodlands

The Hopewell people built burial mounds. The Iroquois League of Five Nations united five groups, including Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, and Seneca. The Cherokee lived farther south and farmed crops including corn, beans, and squash. Eastern Woodland peoples built different homes, including longhouses.

4.5 Missionaries to the American Indians

John Eliot translated the Bible into a Native American language and preached to Native Americans. John Campanius worked with Native Americans near the Delaware River. Roger Williams learned Native languages and sought to communicate with Native peoples. David Brainerd traveled and preached despite illness. These men are described in the textbook as missionaries.

4.6 Famous Woodland Indians

This section reviews historical figures including Joseph Brant, Tecumseh, and Sequoyah. Joseph Brant was a Mohawk leader. Tecumseh was a Shawnee leader who sought alliances among Native peoples. Sequoyah developed a syllabary for writing the Cherokee language, helping Cherokee readers communicate in writing.
