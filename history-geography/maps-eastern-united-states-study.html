Natural Boundaries

Source task 1
Natural boundaries such as mountains, lakes, and rivers determine the shapes and sizes of many eastern states. Locate the map on pp. 346–347 in the student text NWHG and identify these rivers and lakes that serve as parts of state boundaries:

  • Chattahoochee R.
  • Connecticut R.
  • Savannah R.
  • Delaware R.
  • Lake Champlain
  • Ohio R.
  • Lake Ontario
  • Mississippi R.
  • Lake Erie

Source task 2
The states along the Atlantic Coast owe much to the Atlantic Ocean. America’s first great states grew by its bays and harbors. Locate Massachusetts Bay, Chesapeake Bay, and Long Island Sound.

Source task 6
Between Lake Erie and Lake Ontario, the waters of the Niagara River flow over a cliff in a cascade that is one of North America’s great natural wonders—Niagara Falls. Look between the two lakes to find Niagara Falls.

Source task 7
Down the center of Florida runs a long chain of lakes which ends with Lake Okeechobee, one of the largest freshwater lakes within a state. The waters of Lake Okeechobee drain southward through marshes and savannas for 120 miles to the Atlantic, forming the Everglades. Can you locate the Everglades?

Source task 8
Look for the Okefenokee Swamp in Georgia near the Florida border. Like the Everglades, it has become a wildlife preserve.

Source task 9
Look along the coast of North Carolina. The long barrier islands you see there are called the Outer Banks. These sandy reefs form Cape Hatteras and Cape Lookout. Another cape, Cape Fear, is called the “graveyard of the Atlantic” because its shifting sands have caused so many shipwrecks.
