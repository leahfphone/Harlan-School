Geography Facts 2 · Latitude and Climate

Textbook pages 358–359

Parallels of Latitude

Geographers have divided the globe with imaginary lines of latitude and longitude. The parallels of latitude are the lines that run east and west around the globe. The equator divides the Northern Hemisphere from the Southern Hemisphere. The Arctic Circle and the Tropic of Cancer are important lines of latitude in the Northern Hemisphere. Important lines of latitude in the Southern Hemisphere are the Antarctic Circle and the Tropic of Capricorn.

Latitude and Climate

The climate of a region is its characteristic weather conditions over a period of many years. These conditions include temperature and precipitation. Unlike weather, which can change from day to day, the climate of an area remains the same for many years.

Three things help determine the climate of a region: its latitude, its distance from an ocean or sea, and its altitude (height above sea level). Latitude is an extremely important factor.

The region between the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn receives the most sun all year and is called the torrid zone or the Tropics. Torrid means “scorching.” The Tropics are hot most of the time. There are two temperate zones, one between the Tropic of Cancer and the Arctic Circle and the other between the Tropic of Capricorn and the Antarctic Circle. The temperate zones experience the greatest seasonal changes. Temperate means “neither very hot nor very cold.” Between the Arctic Circle and the North Pole and between the Antarctic Circle and the South Pole are the frigid zones. Frigid means “extremely cold.” The frigid zones are cold all year.
