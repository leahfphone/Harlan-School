Geography Facts 3 · Longitude and Time

Textbook pages 360–361

Lines of Longitude

You know that the parallels of latitude run east and west. The lines of longitude run north and south and come together at the North Pole and the South Pole. The lines of longitude are also called meridians. The prime meridian runs through the western parts of Europe and Africa. Its starting point goes through Greenwich, England. The meridians are used to measure distance east or west of the prime meridian.

Degrees of Longitude

Notice the numbers along the equator on the world map on page 362. These numbers show degrees of longitude. Locate 180° longitude. This longitude line is halfway around the world from Greenwich, England, and the prime meridian (0° longitude). Locate the prime meridian and the 180° longitude line on a world globe. Notice how they form a great circle around the earth from the North Pole to the South Pole and back, separating the world into the Eastern Hemisphere and the Western Hemisphere.

Beginning at the prime meridian, the earth is divided by longitude into time zones. Time zone maps help people to know what time it is in other parts of the world.

International Date Line

The International Date Line runs along part of 180° longitude. The International Date Line is jagged so it will not cut through Asia or the possessions of New Zealand. Every midnight, a new day begins at the International Date Line. An hour later, the earth has rotated enough for the same new day to begin at 165° east longitude. In another hour, the same day begins at 150° east longitude. By the time midnight comes to the prime meridian, it is already 12:00 noon at the International Date Line. On the East Coast of the United States, it is still 7:00 P.M. of the previous day, and on the West Coast it is only 4:00 in the afternoon of the previous day.
