CHAPTER 1 — NUMBER SYSTEMS

Whole-number place value

1. DO THIS

  1. Read digits from right to left: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, and so on.
  2. Each place is 10 times the place to its right.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: In 52,407, the 2 means 2,000 and the 4 means 400.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Place value tells what a digit is worth, not just what digit it is.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Check by writing expanded form: 50,000 + 2,000 + 400 + 7.

Add whole numbers

1. DO THIS

  1. Line up the same place values.
  2. Add from the ones place.
  3. Regroup 10 ones as 1 ten whenever needed.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

WORK IT OUT:

  278
+ 156
─────
  434

Explanation: 278 + 156: ones 8+6=14 (write 4, carry 1); tens 7+5+1=13; hundreds 2+1+1=4. Answer 434.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Ten units in one column become one unit in the next column.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Estimate: 280+160≈440.

Subtract whole numbers

1. DO THIS

  1. Align places.
  2. Subtract from the ones place.
  3. If the top digit is smaller, regroup 1 from the next place as 10 in the current place.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

WORK IT OUT:

  402
− 178
─────
  224

Explanation: 402 − 178: regroup 1 hundred into 10 tens, then 1 ten into 10 ones; 12−8=4, 9−7=2, 3−1=2. Answer 224.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Regrouping changes how a number is written, not its value.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

224 + 178 = 402.

Decimal place value

1. DO THIS

  1. Find the decimal point.
  2. Read places to its right: tenths, hundredths, thousandths.
  3. Each place is one tenth of the place to its left.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 3.047 = 3 ones + 0 tenths + 4 hundredths + 7 thousandths.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

A decimal digit has a different value depending on its place.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Use zeros as placeholders; 3.047 is not 3.47.

Add and subtract decimals

1. DO THIS

  1. Line up decimal points.
  2. Add zeros if helpful.
  3. Compute by columns.
  4. Bring the decimal straight down.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 12.5 − 3.78 = 12.50 − 3.78 = 8.72.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Aligning decimals lines up equal place values.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Estimate: 12.5−3.8≈8.7.

Positive and negative numbers

1. DO THIS

  1. On a number line, right is greater; left is smaller.
  2. For same-sign addition, add magnitudes and keep sign.
  3. For opposite signs, subtract magnitudes and keep sign of larger magnitude.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: −7 + 3 = −4; −2 − 5 = −7.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Negative numbers represent positions below zero or amounts owed.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Sketch a number line.

Roman numerals

1. DO THIS

  1. I=1, V=5, X=10, L=50, C=100, D=500, M=1,
  2. Usually add symbols left to right; a smaller symbol before a larger one can mean subtract (IV, IX, XL, XC, CD, CM).

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: XIV = 10 + 4 = 14; XL = 40.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Roman numerals use symbols and grouping instead of place-value digits.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Check valid subtractive pairs.

Number patterns

1. DO THIS

  1. Compare consecutive terms.
  2. Look for a repeated add/subtract/multiply/divide rule.
  3. Apply the same rule to find missing terms.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 4, 8, 16, 32, __ → multiply by 2 → 64.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

A pattern is a rule that stays consistent.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Test your rule on every pair, not only the first.

CHAPTER 2 — MULTIPLYING BY A WHOLE NUMBER

Multiply whole numbers

1. DO THIS

  1. Line up digits by place value.
  2. Multiply each digit of the top number by each digit of the bottom number.
  3. Shift each partial product according to its place.
  4. Add partial products.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

WORK IT OUT:

   24
×   13
──────

72 (24 × 3)

+ 240 (24 × 10)

──────
  312

Explanation: 34 × 12 = (34×10)+(34×2)=340+68=408.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Multiplication distributes across place values.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Estimate 30×10≈300; check with division.

Multiply by powers of 10

1. DO THIS

  1. For whole numbers, multiplying by 10, 100, or 1,000 makes each digit move 1, 2, or 3 places left in value.
  2. Add placeholder zeros when needed.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 46 × 100 = 4,600.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Every digit becomes 100 times as valuable.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

For decimals, shift place values—not blindly add zeros.

Exponents

1. DO THIS

  1. Identify base and exponent.
  2. Multiply the base by itself the exponent number of times.
  3. Follow order of operations.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 3⁴ = 3×3×3×3 = 81 (not 3×4).

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

An exponent counts equal factors.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

3²=9; compare with 3×2=6.

Multiply decimals by a whole number

1. DO THIS

  1. Multiply as whole numbers.
  2. Place the decimal so the product has the same number of decimal places as the decimal factor.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 2.35 × 4: 235×4=940 → 9.40 = 9.4.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Each hundredth is multiplied; 940 hundredths equals 9.40.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Estimate 2.4×4≈9.6.

3-digit multipliers

1. DO THIS

  1. Multiply by ones.
  2. Multiply by tens (shift one place).
  3. Multiply by hundreds (shift two places).
  4. Add.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 123 × 204 = 123×200 + 123×4 = 24,600+492 = 25,092.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Each partial product represents a different place-value group.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Estimate 120×200≈24,000.

Squares and square roots

1. DO THIS

  1. Square means multiply a number by itself.
  2. A square root asks which nonnegative number squared gives the original number.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 9²=81; √81=9.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Squaring and taking the principal square root undo each other for nonnegative numbers.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Check 9×9=81.

CHAPTER 3 — DIVIDING BY A WHOLE NUMBER

Division vocabulary and fact families

1. DO THIS

  1. Dividend ÷ divisor = quotient.
  2. A remainder is what cannot form another full group.
  3. Use multiplication to check.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 37 ÷ 5 = 7 R2 because 5×7+2=37.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Division finds equal groups or the size of each group.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Remainder must be smaller than divisor.

Long division: one-digit divisor

1. DO THIS

  1. Start at the leftmost place that can make a full group.
  2. Divide.
  3. Multiply.
  4. Subtract.
  5. Regroup/bring down the next digit.
  6. Repeat.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

WORK IT OUT:

25 r3

______

5 ) 128

   −10

───

     28
    −25

───

      3

Explanation: 364 ÷ 7: 36 tens ÷ 7 = 5 tens, 1 ten left; regroup to 14 ones; 14÷7=2. Answer 52.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Bringing down means regrouping a remainder into the next smaller place value.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

7×52=364.

Zero in the quotient

1. DO THIS

  1. When a place value cannot make even one group, write 0 in that quotient place and continue.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

WORK IT OUT:

      105

______

9 ) 945

   −9

──

     04
     −0

──

      45
     −45

──

       0

Explanation: 945 ÷ 9 = 105, not 15. After 9 hundreds ÷ 9, 4 tens cannot make 9 groups, so the tens digit is 0.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Zero holds the place so hundreds do not turn into tens.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

9×105=945.

Divisibility rules

1. DO THIS

  1. 2: even last digit. 3: digit sum divisible by
  2. 4: last two digits divisible by
  3. 5: ends 0 or
  4. 6: divisible by 2 AND
  5. 9: digit sum divisible by
  6. 10: ends 0.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 1,332: even; 1+3+3+2=9, so divisible by 2, 3, 6, and 9.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Tens and hundreds have predictable remainders when divided by these numbers.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Test both parts of the rule for 6.

Divide by a multiple of 10

1. DO THIS

  1. If dividend and divisor both end in 0, divide BOTH by 10 (or 100, etc.).
  2. Divide the smaller numbers.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

WORK IT OUT:

420 ÷ 60

42 ÷ 6

───────
      7

Explanation: 420 ÷ 60 = 42 ÷ 6 = 7.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Dividing both numbers by the same nonzero amount preserves the quotient.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Never cancel a zero from only one number.

Two-digit divisors

1. DO THIS

  1. Round divisor to estimate.
  2. Use actual divisor to multiply.
  3. If product is too large, lower the quotient digit.
  4. Subtract and continue.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

WORK IT OUT:

156 r14

________

24 ) 3758

    −24

──

     135
    −120

───

      158
     −144

───

       14

Explanation: 158 ÷ 24: 24×7=168 (too high); 24×6=144; remainder 14 → 6 R14.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Rounding suggests a guess; exact multiplication confirms it.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

24×6+14=158.

Division with decimals in the dividend

1. DO THIS

  1. Divide as usual.
  2. When reaching the decimal in the dividend, put the decimal directly above it in the quotient.
  3. Continue dividing, adding trailing zeros if needed.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 12.6 ÷ 3 = 4.2.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Each decimal place is divided just like a whole-number place.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

3×4.2=12.6.

Order of operations

1. DO THIS

  1. Grouping symbols.
  2. Exponents.
  3. Multiplication/division left to right.
  4. Addition/subtraction left to right.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 18 ÷ 3 × 2 = 6×2 = 12, not 3.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Multiplication and division have equal priority; work left to right.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Write each intermediate step.

Multistep word problems

1. DO THIS

  1. Read twice.
  2. Underline what is asked.
  3. Label known quantities with units.
  4. Plan operations.
  5. Solve and interpret any remainder.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 43 sandwiches, 4 per tray → 43÷4=10 R3; need 11 trays to hold all sandwiches.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

The meaning of a remainder depends on the question.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Ask whether to round up, ignore, or report remainder.

CHAPTER 4 — FRACTION THEORY

Greatest common factor (GCF)

1. DO THIS

  1. List factors of each number or prime-factorize.
  2. Find common factors.
  3. Choose the largest.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: Factors of 12: 1,2,3,4,6,12; of 18: 1,2,3,6,9,18 → GCF=6.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

GCF finds the largest equal size that fits both numbers.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

6 divides both 12 and 18.

Least common multiple (LCM)

1. DO THIS

  1. List multiples or prime-factorize.
  2. Find the first positive common multiple.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: Multiples of 4: 4,8,12; of 6: 6,12 → LCM=12.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

LCM finds when repeating groups meet.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

12÷4 and 12÷6 are whole numbers.

Proper and improper fractions

1. DO THIS

  1. Numerator counts parts; denominator names equal parts per whole.
  2. Proper: numerator smaller than denominator.
  3. Improper: numerator at least denominator.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 7/4 = 1 3/4 because 7÷4=1 R3.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Four fourths make one whole.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

1 3/4 = (1×4+3)/4 = 7/4.

Equivalent fractions and simplification

1. DO THIS

  1. Multiply or divide numerator AND denominator by the same nonzero number.
  2. To simplify, divide by their GCF.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 12/18 = (12÷6)/(18÷6) = 2/3.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Both numbers change by the same factor, so the fraction value stays equal.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Cross multiply: 12×3=18×2.

Compare and order fractions

1. DO THIS

  1. Find a common denominator, OR cross multiply for two fractions with positive denominators.
  2. Compare numerators.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 3/4 vs 5/8 → 6/8 > 5/8, so 3/4 > 5/8.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Equal-sized parts can be compared directly.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Use benchmark 1/2 or 1.

Fractions and percents

1. DO THIS

  1. Percent means out of
  2. Convert fraction to decimal by division, then multiply by 100 to get percent.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 3/4 = 0.75 = 75%.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

75% means 75 of every 100 equal parts.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

75/100 simplifies to 3/4.

CHAPTER 5 — ADDING & SUBTRACTING FRACTIONS

Estimate fraction sums and differences

1. DO THIS

  1. Round to benchmarks such as 0, 1/2, or 1, then calculate exactly.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 7/8 + 1/3 ≈ 1 + 1/2 = 1 1/2.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

An estimate helps catch unreasonable answers.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Exact sum 1 5/24 is near the estimate.

Like denominators

1. DO THIS

  1. Keep denominator.
  2. Add or subtract numerators.
  3. Simplify.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

The pieces are the same size; only the number of pieces changes.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Never add denominators.

Unlike denominators

1. DO THIS

  1. Find LCM of denominators.
  2. Rewrite equivalent fractions.
  3. Add/subtract numerators.
  4. Simplify.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 1/3 + 1/4 = 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

You must count pieces of the same size.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Estimate 1/3+1/4≈1/2.

Mixed numbers

1. DO THIS

  1. Add/subtract whole parts and fractional parts, regrouping if needed; OR convert to improper fractions.
  2. Simplify.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 2 1/4 − 3/4 = 1 5/4 − 3/4 = 1 2/4 = 1 1/2.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Borrowing 1 whole creates 4 fourths.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

1 1/2 + 3/4 = 2 1/4.

CHAPTER 6 — PLANE FIGURE GEOMETRY

Points, lines, segments, rays

1. DO THIS

  1. Point: location.
  2. Line: extends forever both ways.
  3. Segment: two endpoints.
  4. Ray: one endpoint, extends forever one way.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: A segment joining A and B has two endpoints.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Geometry names different kinds of straight paths.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Draw arrowheads only where a figure extends.

Parallel, perpendicular, intersecting

1. DO THIS

  1. Parallel lines in a plane never meet.
  2. Perpendicular lines meet at 90°.
  3. Intersecting lines cross (not necessarily at 90°).

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: Railroad tracks: parallel; square corner: perpendicular.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Angles and intersections describe how lines relate.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Check for a right-angle marker.

Angles and relationships

1. DO THIS

  1. Acute <90°; right =90°; obtuse >90° and <180°; straight =180°.
  2. Complementary sum 90°; supplementary sum 180°.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: Angle 35° has complement 55° and supplement 145°.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Angles combine to form larger known angles.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

35+55=90; 35+145=180.

Polygons, triangles, quadrilaterals

1. DO THIS

  1. Polygon: closed straight-sided figure.
  2. Triangle: 3 sides, angles total 180°.
  3. Quadrilateral: 4 sides, angles total 360°.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: Triangle angles 50° and 60° → third 70°.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Interior angle totals let you find missing angles.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

50+60+70=180.

Congruent, similar, transformations

1. DO THIS

  1. Congruent: same shape and size.
  2. Similar: same shape, possibly different size.
  3. Translation slides; reflection flips; rotation turns.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: Two squares with side lengths 2 and 4 are similar, not congruent.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Similarity preserves angles and proportional side lengths.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Check matching angles and side ratios.

Circles and 3D figures

1. DO THIS

  1. Radius: center to circle; diameter=2×radius.
  2. Circumference=πd; area=πr². 3D solids have faces, edges, vertices (when applicable).

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: Radius 3 → diameter 6; circumference 6π; area 9π.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Circumference measures distance around; area measures space inside.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Label units: length vs square units.

CHAPTER 7 — MULTIPLYING FRACTIONS & DECIMALS

Multiply fractions

1. DO THIS

  1. Multiply numerators.
  2. Multiply denominators.
  3. Simplify (you may cross-cancel common factors first).

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 2/3 × 3/5 = 6/15 = 2/5.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

A fraction of a fraction multiplies the two proportions.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Result of two positive proper fractions is smaller than each.

Multiply mixed numbers

1. DO THIS

  1. Convert to improper fractions.
  2. Multiply numerators and denominators.
  3. Simplify and convert back if needed.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 1 1/2 × 2/3 = 3/2 × 2/3 = 1.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Improper fractions let you use the same multiplication rule.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Estimate 1.5×0.67≈1.

Multiply decimals

1. DO THIS

  1. Multiply ignoring decimals.
  2. Count total decimal places in both factors.
  3. Put that many decimal places in the product.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 1.2 × 0.35: 12×35=420; 1+2=3 decimal places → 0.420 = 0.42.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Tenths × hundredths = thousandths.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Estimate 1×0.35≈0.35.

Numbers between numbers

1. DO THIS

  1. Write numbers with equal decimal places or common denominators.
  2. Choose a value strictly between them.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: Between 0.4 and 0.5: 0.45.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

You can always divide the gap into smaller equal pieces.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

0.40 < 0.45 < 0.50.

CHAPTER 8 — DIVIDING FRACTIONS

Divide by a fraction

1. DO THIS

  1. Keep first number.
  2. Change ÷ to ×.
  3. Flip the second fraction (reciprocal).
  4. Multiply and simplify.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 3/4 × 2/1 = 3/2 = 1 1/2.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Dividing by 1/2 asks how many halves fit; multiplying by 2 counts them.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

(1 1/2) × (1/2) = 3/4.

Reciprocals and mixed numbers

1. DO THIS

  1. A nonzero fraction a/b has reciprocal b/a.
  2. Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions before dividing.
  3. Never divide by zero.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 2 1/4 ÷ 3/4 = 9/4 × 4/3 = 3.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

A nonzero number multiplied by its reciprocal equals 1.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

3 × 3/4 = 2 1/4.

Multistep equations

1. DO THIS

  1. Simplify each side using order of operations.
  2. Undo addition/subtraction, then multiplication/division as appropriate.
  3. Check by substitution.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 3x + 4 = 19 → 3x=15 → x=5.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Inverse operations keep an equation balanced when applied to both sides.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

3×5+4=19.

Mathematical properties

1. DO THIS

  1. Commutative: a+b=b+a and ab=ba.
  2. Associative: (a+b)+c=a+(b+c).
  3. Distributive: a(b+c)=ab+ac.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 6(10+2)=60+12=72.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Properties let us regroup or expand without changing value.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Subtraction and division are NOT commutative.

CHAPTER 9 — DIVIDING DECIMALS

Decimal dividend, whole-number divisor

1. DO THIS

  1. Divide normally.
  2. Place decimal in quotient above decimal in dividend.
  3. Add trailing zeros to continue if necessary.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 8.4 ÷ 2 = 4.2.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

You are sharing tenths just like ones.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

4.2×2=8.4.

Decimal divisor

1. DO THIS

  1. Multiply divisor by 10, 100, etc. until it is a whole number.
  2. Multiply dividend by the SAME amount.
  3. Divide.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 6.3 ÷ 0.3 = 63 ÷ 3 = 21.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Scaling both numbers equally keeps the quotient unchanged.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

21×0.3=6.3.

Repeating decimals and rounding

1. DO THIS

  1. Divide to desired place.
  2. If digits repeat in a cycle, write repeating bar if instructed.
  3. To round, look one place right of desired place; 5–9 round up.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 1 ÷ 3 = 0.333…; to hundredths, 0.33.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Some fractions do not terminate in base ten.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

Check rounding place and units.

Fractions as decimals

1. DO THIS

  1. Divide numerator by denominator; use zeros after decimal to continue.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 3/8 = 3÷8 = 0.375.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

A fraction is a division expression.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

0.375×8=3.

Real numbers

1. DO THIS

  1. Rational numbers can be written as a/b (b≠0), including terminating/repeating decimals.
  2. Irrational numbers cannot, e.g., √2 and π.
  3. Both are real numbers.

2. WORKED EXAMPLE

EXAMPLE: 0.75 = 3/4 rational; √2 irrational.

3. WHY DOES IT WORK?

Real numbers include rational and irrational values on the number line.

4. CHECK YOUR ANSWER

A nonrepeating, nonterminating decimal is irrational.
